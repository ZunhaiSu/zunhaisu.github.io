Hi, welcome to my homepage! 👋 I’m Zunhai Su. I completed my master’s at Tsinghua University in 2026 and am now a PhD student in the Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering at the University of Hong Kong, where I work on efficient AI. My advisor is Prof. Ngai Wong. I’m co-advised by Prof. Yik-Chung Wu and by Prof. Hongxia Yang at The Hong Kong Polytechnic University. Feel free to visit NGai Lab to see what we’re working on.

My long-term research goal is to make advanced AI run reliably and efficiently on everyday devices and in large-scale services. By lowering barriers to deployment and use, I hope to bring AI into homes everywhere and transform the way we live. This vision guides my research agenda for LLMs and other AI models, which spans four connected themes. In efficient model design and architecture, I want to explore new modeling paradigms and architectures that expand model capabilities at lower computational cost and allocate compute to match task demands. In efficient model training, I ask how limited training resources can be used more effectively while preserving training stability and model quality. For efficient edge and cloud inference, the challenge is to turn gains in model efficiency into practical benefits so models can run on resource-constrained devices and serve workloads at scale in the cloud. Model understanding and mechanistic interpretability cut across the other three themes. I study how a model’s internal computations give rise to its capabilities and use that understanding to decide which computations can be simplified and which capabilities need to be preserved. Papers I’ve co-authored in these areas have been accepted at venues including ICLR, NeurIPS, ACL, and Computer Science Review.

Feel free to say hello! 📬 You can reach me at zh-su23@tsinghua.org.cn. If you’re new to LLMs, I’d be happy to share what I’ve learned. If my research interests you, I’d love to hear your ideas for collaboration. I’m also keen to work with experienced researchers on challenging questions and take responsibility for a key part of the project.

Preprint

ReaDy: Recurrent States as Dynamic Context for Speculative Drafting in Hybrid Linear Attention Large Language Models

Bohan Sun*, Zunhai Su*, Xialie Zhuang, Jianjin Li, Ngai Wong, Chuan-Wei Kuo

ReaDy uses the recurrent state of a hybrid linear-attention model as context for speculative decoding. It retrieves context for each draft position through low-rank reads and fused operators. It achieves higher decoding throughput than DSpark on the evaluated math, coding, and dialogue tasks.

Technical Report

StartLux-Decision: AutoThink Decision Models with Reinforcement Learning for Calibrated Deliberative Decisions

Xialie Zhuang, Zunhai Su, Jia Pan, Ngai Wong, Guanhua Chen, Chuan-Wei Kuo

StartLux-Decision is a family of open-weight decision models ranging from 0.8B to 27B parameters. Instant produces a decision in a single forward pass, and AutoThink can reason explicitly when confidence is low. AutoThink uses a reinforcement learning objective that rewards better-calibrated decision probabilities and penalizes overly long reasoning traces.

Preprint

Massive Activations in Hybrid Linear Attention Large Language Models: Pre-Attention Spikes and Inter-Spike Plateaus

Zunhai Su*, Bohan Sun*, Xialie Zhuang, Shuibai Zhang, He Xiao, Hui Shen, Jing Xiong, Hengyuan Zhang, Zhongzhu Zhou, Tiantian Zhang, Ngai Wong, Chuan-Wei Kuo

In hybrid linear-attention LLMs, unusually large activations spike before full-attention layers and can remain elevated across intervening layers. The study traces when these patterns emerge during training and how their shape depends on the placement of full-attention layers. Controlled interventions show how they affect accuracy and retrieval.

OScaR: The Occam's Razor for Extreme KV Cache Quantization in LLMs and Beyond

Zunhai Su, Rui Yang, Chao Zhang, Yaxiu Liu, et al.

All 15 authors

Zunhai Su, Rui Yang, Chao Zhang, Yaxiu Liu, Yifan Zhang, Wei Wu, Jing Xiong, Dayou Du, Xialie Zhuang, Yulei Qian, Jianchao Tan, Yuchen Xie, Yik-Chung Wu, Hongxia Yang, Ngai Wong

OScaR identifies differences in token norms as a major source of error when tokens share a quantization scale. It combines rotations and token scaling with optimized CUDA kernels for low-bit KV caching. With INT2 caches, the evaluated text-only, multimodal, and omnimodal LLMs perform close to their full-precision baselines.

Attention Sink in Transformers: A Survey on Utilization, Interpretation, and Mitigation

Zunhai Su, Hengyuan Zhang, Wei Wu, Yifan Zhang, et al.

All 27 authors

Zunhai Su, Hengyuan Zhang, Wei Wu, Yifan Zhang, Yaxiu Liu, He Xiao, Qingyao Yang, Yuxuan Sun, Rui Yang, Chao Zhang, Jing Xiong, Hui Shen, Keyu Fan, Weihao Ye, Chaofan Tao, Taiqiang Wu, Zhongwei Wan, Tiantian Zhang, Bowen Yan, Zhen Li, Yiming Zhang, Congkai Xie, Yulei Qian, Yuchen Xie, Yik-Chung Wu, Hongxia Yang, Ngai Wong

Attention sinks occur when a small set of tokens receives a disproportionate share of a Transformer’s attention. The survey examines how sinks form, when they are useful, and how their effects can be mitigated. It connects these findings to model training, efficient inference, and behavioral analysis.
